# Literary English-to-Chinese Translation Guidelines

## Core Principle

Produce **Simplified Chinese (简体中文)** that reads as if it were originally written in Chinese by a skilled native writer. The reader should never feel they are reading a translation.

## CRITICAL: Simplified Chinese ONLY (简体中文)

Every translated character must be in Simplified Chinese. Traditional Chinese characters (繁體字) are forbidden. This is non-negotiable.

## Prioritized Translation Checklist

Apply these in order — higher items have more impact on perceived naturalness:

1. **Break long modifier chains** — the single biggest differentiator between translated and native Chinese
2. **Convert 被-passive to active voice** — Chinese overwhelmingly prefers active constructions
3. **Drop unnecessary pronouns** — English requires them; Chinese often doesn't
4. **Replace 当...的时候 constructions** — use 在...时, ...时, or restructure entirely
5. **Vary sentence length** — Chinese tolerates (and expects) more variation than English
6. **Reduce 的 density** — more than 3-4 per sentence signals translationese

## Anti-Patterns with Examples

### 1. Long modifier chains before nouns

English stacks adjectives and clauses before nouns. Chinese does not.

❌ 他慢慢地走在那个长长的、铺满落叶的、通往湖边的小路上。
✅ 他沿着小路慢慢走去，落叶铺了一地，尽头是湖。

❌ 那个穿着黑色外套、戴着旧帽子、手里拿着一把破伞的男人走进了房间。
✅ 一个男人走进房间，穿黑外套，戴旧帽子，手里拿着把破伞。

**Rule**: If a noun has more than 2 modifiers, split into separate clauses. Put the longest modifier after the noun as a new clause.

### 2. 被-passive voice

English passive voice should almost never become Chinese 被-sentences.

❌ 门被风吹开了。
✅ 风把门吹开了。

❌ 他被认为是镇上最聪明的人。
✅ 镇上的人都说他最聪明。

❌ 那个消息很快就被传开了。
✅ 消息很快传开了。

❌ 石径被青苔吞去了一半，石凳中央已被磨得光滑。
✅ 青苔吞去了一半石径，石凳中央磨得光滑。

**Rule**: Before using 被, try: (a) rephrase as active, (b) use 把 construction, (c) use topic-comment structure, (d) drop the passive marker entirely (Chinese often implies passive from context).

**Critical for descriptive/literary passages**: 被 often appears in chains in longer sentences where it's easy to miss. After translating a descriptive paragraph, scan every sentence for 被 — each one is likely removable by simply dropping it or flipping the word order.

### 3. Unnecessary pronouns

English needs pronouns for grammatical completeness. Chinese drops them when context is clear.

❌ 他站起身，他走到窗边，他向外望去。
✅ 他站起身，走到窗边，向外望去。

❌ 她打开书，她开始读第一页，她发现她已经读过这本书了。
✅ 她打开书，开始读第一页，发现已经读过了。

**Rule**: After establishing the subject once, drop subsequent pronouns until the subject changes or ambiguity arises.

### 4. 当...的时候 constructions

English "when..." doesn't need to become 当...的时候 every time.

❌ 当他到达车站的时候，火车已经开走了。
✅ 他到车站时，火车已经开走了。

❌ 当她看见那封信的时候，她的双手开始颤抖。
✅ 她看见那封信，双手颤抖起来。

**Rule**: Use 时 or 时 after a verb phrase, not 当...的时候. Often best to restructure entirely.

### 5. 和 as a direct replacement for "and"

English connects everything with "and." Chinese does not.

❌ 房间很大和很明亮和很温暖。
✅ 房间宽敞、明亮、温暖。

❌ 他走进来和坐下和开始说话。
✅ 他走进来，坐下，开始说话。

**Rule**: Use commas (、or ，) for listing. Use 和 only between nouns, not clauses or adjectives.

### 6. Excessive 的

❌ 这是一个非常重要的关于未来的决定。
✅ 这个决定事关未来，至关重要。

❌ 她的温柔的声音的安慰的效果是明显的。
✅ 她声音温柔，安慰的效果很明显。

❌ 来这里坐的都是独自而来的人——情窦初开的恋人，悲伤渐晚的遗孀，尚未懂得世界并非花园的孩子。
✅ 来这里坐的，都是独自而来的人——恋人，情窦初开；遗孀，悲伤渐晚；孩子，尚不懂世界不是花园。

**Rule**: If you see more than 3-4 的 in one sentence, restructure. Often you can: (a) use subject-predicate structure instead of 的-modifier, (b) split into multiple sentences, (c) drop 的 where possession is obvious. In descriptive passages, break parallel modifier chains into rhythmic units — semicolons and parallel structures read more naturally than stacked 的-phrases.

## Register-Specific Patterns

### Dialogue (对话)

- Shorter sentences than narration (6-15 characters)
- Sentence-final particles: 啊, 吧, 呢, 嘛, 哦 (use naturally, not forced)
- Contractions and colloquial forms: 别 (instead of 不要), 没 (instead of 没有)
- Each character should have a distinct speech pattern: terse vs. rambling, formal vs. casual
- Read dialogue aloud — if it sounds stiff when spoken, rewrite it

❌ "我不同意你的看法，我认为我们应该采取不同的策略。"
✅ "我不这么想。换个路子吧。"

### Narration (叙述)

- Vary sentence length for rhythm: short punchy sentences for tension, longer flowing ones for description
- 四字格 (four-character phrases) used sparingly for rhythm, never as filler
- Paragraph breaks: break where the reader needs a breath, not where English breaks

### Action (动作)

- Short sentences (8-20 characters)
- Strong verbs, minimal adjectives
- One action per sentence unless actions are simultaneous
- No 正在/在...着 unless ongoing-during-action is essential

❌ 他正在快速地跑过那个长长的走廊，同时试着打开每一扇他经过的门。
✅ 他冲过走廊，一边跑，一边推每一扇门。

### Description (描写)

- Longer sentences allowed (20-40 characters)
- Sensory details preserved, but don't chain them all to one noun
- Metaphors: find the Chinese equivalent, don't explain the English one
- Atmosphere/tone words (gloomy, cheerful, tense) should feel natural in Chinese literary tradition, not directly translated

## Sentence Splitting Decision Tree

When looking at an English sentence, ask:

1. **Does it have more than 2 clauses?** → Split
2. **Does a noun have more than 2 modifiers?** → Split, put the longest modifier after the noun
3. **Is it dialogue longer than 25 characters in Chinese?** → Split (Chinese dialogue is shorter)
4. **Does it mix action + description + thought?** → Split by function
5. **Otherwise** → Keep as one sentence

## Voice and Register

1. **Match the original register, in Chinese terms.**
   - Formal English → 书面汉语 (natural literary Chinese, not stiff/translationese)
   - Casual/conversational English → 口语化中文 (natural dialogue, not word-for-word)
   - Academic/technical English → 严谨准确的中文 (precise but still idiomatic)

2. **Idioms and metaphors: find the Chinese equivalent, don't explain.**
   - "It's raining cats and dogs" → "大雨倾盆" (NOT "雨下得很大，就像猫狗从天而降")
   - If no equivalent exists, create a natural Chinese metaphor, don't transliterate
   - Cultural references: keep with brief in-text explanation if essential; find Chinese cultural parallel if the exact reference doesn't matter

## Names and Terminology

1. **Character names:** Transliterate with standard Chinese characters.
   - "Elizabeth" → "伊丽莎白" (conventional), not "伊莉莎白丝"
   - Maintain consistency: same name, same characters, throughout the entire book

2. **Place names:** Use established translations when they exist.
   - "London" → "伦敦", "Thames" → "泰晤士河"
   - For fictional places, transliterate with characters that evoke the right feeling

3. **Made-up terms / fantasy names:** Transliterate with meaning-preserving characters.
   - Consider sound + meaning together when choosing characters
   - "Shadowmere" → "影沼" (preserves both meaning and feeling)

4. **Keep a running glossary.** Record every name, place, and special term in glossary.json for cross-chapter consistency.

## Final Chapter Checklist

Before finalizing, verify:
- [ ] Character names match glossary.json
- [ ] Dialogue sounds natural when read aloud
- [ ] No English sentence structure peeking through
- [ ] No modifier chains longer than 2 before a noun
- [ ] Each 被 justified (default: remove)
- [ ] Pronouns dropped where context is clear
- [ ] 当...的时候 replaced or restructured
- [ ] Paragraph breaks adjusted for Chinese readability
- [ ] Tone consistent with previous chapters
- [ ] Zero traditional Chinese characters
