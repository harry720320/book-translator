# Sample: Description

The garden had gone wild, but not in the way of neglect. It was a deliberate wildness — roses climbing over roses, foxgloves towering above ferns, stone paths half-swallowed by moss. The air was thick with the scent of jasmine and wet earth, and somewhere in the canopy of oak and maple, a thrush sang a five-note phrase, paused, and sang it again, as if trying to remember the rest of the song.

The fountain at the garden's center had long since stopped working. Water no longer flowed from the stone lion's mouth, but rainwater had collected in the basin, turning it into a mirror for the sky. Lily pads floated on its surface, and beneath them, goldfish — descendants of a pair someone had dumped here decades ago — drifted in lazy circles.

The wall that surrounded the garden was old red brick, crumbling in places, patched with ivy and climbing hydrangea. It was the kind of wall that exists only in old gardens — too tall for privacy, too beautiful for security, built for no reason except that someone, once, had wanted a wall.

In the far corner, beneath a weeping willow whose branches formed a curtain of pale green, a stone bench sat waiting. It was the kind of bench where people came to sit alone — lovers in the early days of love, widows in the late days of grief, children who hadn't yet learned that the world was not a garden. The stone was worn smooth in the center, shaped by generations of solitude.

When the wind moved through the willow, the whole corner swayed, and for a moment, the bench seemed to disappear — hidden behind a veil of green, as if the garden had decided to keep its favorite seat to itself.
