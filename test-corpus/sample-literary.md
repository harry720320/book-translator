# Sample: Literary Fiction

The house had been empty for as long as anyone could remember, but the lights still came on at dusk — not from any switch or fuse, but from something older, something that remembered the family who had once lived there. The locals kept their distance, and the children, cruel in the way only children can be, dared each other to touch the front gate and run.

Eleanor had never been one for dares. She was a woman who measured things — flour by the gram, silence by the second, grief by the year. When she pulled up to the house in her dusty blue sedan, she had no intention of running. She had come to measure the house itself, to see if it was worth what the county assessor claimed, and she had brought her tools: a clipboard, a flashlight, and a heart so carefully guarded that even she had forgotten what it held.

The front gate swung open before she touched it.

Inside, the air was thick with the scent of old wood and something sweeter — lilacs, she thought, though it was the wrong season. The floorboards creaked under her weight, but not in the way old houses creak. They creaked in welcome. Eleanor noticed this the way she noticed everything: as data, not portent.

She was halfway through the parlor, measuring tape stretched between her fingers, when she heard the piano. It came from upstairs, a melody she almost recognized — something her grandmother might have hummed, or something she'd heard in a dream. The notes were clear and deliberate, as if whoever played them had all the time in the world.

Eleanor wrote in her clipboard: *Structural assessment inconclusive. Recommend revisiting after dark.*
