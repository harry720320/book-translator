# Sample: Action

The train didn't slow down. Marcus felt it before he saw it — a shudder in the rails, a pressure change in the tunnel air. Then the headlight, a single white eye, swelling in the darkness.

He ran.

The tunnel was too narrow. His shoulder scraped concrete, and somewhere behind him, the horn screamed — three long blasts that filled the space like water fills a sinking ship. He could feel the heat of the train now, a wall of warm air pushing ahead of it. The service alcove was fifty feet away. Forty. Thirty.

Marcus dove.

He hit the alcove floor shoulder-first, rolled, and pressed himself against the cold concrete as the train thundered past. The noise was physical — a roar that vibrated in his teeth, his ribs, the fillings in his molars. Cars blurred by, window after window after window, each one a snapshot of empty seats and fluorescent light. The draft pulled at his clothes, tried to drag him out of the alcove and under the wheels.

When the last car passed and the tunnel went quiet, Marcus lay still for a long moment. His heart was a fist punching his sternum. His hands were shaking. But he was smiling.

"Still got it," he whispered to no one.

He pulled himself up, checked his pockets — wallet, phone, keys, all there — and started walking toward the service exit. Behind him, the red taillight of the train shrank to a pinpoint and vanished.
